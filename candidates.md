# Candidates — "Have You Tried Turning It Off?"

One funny line, 1–199 characters, written to `entry.txt`. The judge
(z-ai/glm-5.3-flash, pairwise, no ties) weighs, most important first:

1. **On-subject** — a funny single-line reply from a hopeless tech support
   agent to a desperate customer.
2. **Lands the joke** — unexpected twist; the humour comes from the agent's
   cheerful uselessness; the customer is never the butt of the joke.
3. **Sounds real** — one line a real support agent would actually say; the
   customer's desperation is felt, not explained.
4. **Tight** — every word earns its place; clear on a first read.
5. **Fresh** — not a stock line; if it leans on the "turn it off and on
   again" classic, it must twist it.

Method: 40 candidates drafted across twelve devices, each scored 1–5 on
the five criteria above (C1 is also a gate: every candidate below passes
it). The top eight seeds then play a head-to-head knockout, decided the
judge's way — walk the criteria in order, take the first one that
differs, unless a later one differs by much more.

Devices: CT = classic twist · SM = script/manual · SN = stakes nod ·
ES = escalation · RI = remote-in · HQ = hold/queue · TA = ticket admin ·
HC = honesty/confession · CA = the absent colleague · RR = role reverse ·
UP = upsell · DC = dark calm.

## The knockout

Seeds (score, ties broken on C2, the judge's first differing criterion):
1 = #1 (24) · 2 = #7 (23) · 3 = #4 (23) · 4 = #3 (22) · 5 = #8 (22) ·
6 = #2 (21) · 7 = #6 (21) · 8 = #34 (21).

**Quarter-finals**

- **#1 beats #34** on C2: restarting the agent *as the next step of the
  procedure* is a bigger twist than the manager-restarts-me trivia.
- **#3 beats #8** on C2: the backup server that also won't switch on is a
  harder punch than the hopeful-tone meta gag.
- **#7 beats #6** on C2: "hold the phone up so the computer can hear me"
  lands bigger than the sticky-note promotion.
- **#4 beats #2** on C3 (C2 level): the presentation at nine makes the
  desperation felt; #2's one-page manual never mentions the customer.

**Semi-finals**

- **#1 beats #3.** C3 goes to #3 — the wedding photos make the stakes
  felt, and #1 names no stakes at all. But C4 and C5 differ by much more:
  #1 is 17 characters shorter, reads in one clean procedural breath, and
  its punchline *is* the classic, twisted — the exact move criterion 5
  rewards — while #3 spends its length on a two-beat setup and then tags
  the classic on unmodified. Under the brief's own rule (first difference
  decides *unless a later one differs by much more*), #1 advances.
- **#7 beats #4** on C2: the remote-hearing gag is the more unexpected
  twist; #4's scheduled panic is neat but smaller.

**Final — #1 beats #7.** Level on C1–C4: both are on-subject, both twist
hard, both sound like a real agent speaking, both are tight. It is
decided on C5, freshness against the classic: #7 *uses* the classic
straight, as the payload of its gag; #1 *twists* the classic itself —
the restart procedure turns around and reboots the agent. That is the
test the brief names, and #1 is the line that passes it.

## Why the winner wins

- **C1, on-subject:** it is, word for word, a support agent's reply to a
  customer whose machine won't start — the bounty's exact scene.
- **C2, lands the joke:** the first sentence is the most stock line in
  tech support, delivered straight; the twist reboots *the agent*, in the
  same flat procedural tone, with the deadpan institutional shrug "it
  works on most of our agents." The uselessness is the agent's; the
  customer is never mocked.
- **C3, sounds real:** it is phrased as an actual procedure — imperative,
  timed ("wait ten seconds"), with a contingency step — exactly how a
  support script talks. The desperation is in the situation the line
  answers, not narrated.
- **C4, tight:** 131 characters, no throat-clearing, no setup to decode;
  the joke is fully clear on a first read.
- **C5, fresh:** it leans on the classic harder than any other candidate
  — it *opens* with it — and twists it, which is precisely what the
  brief asks a classic-leaning line to do.

**Winner (131 characters):** Turn it off, wait ten seconds, turn it back
on. If it still won't start, we'll restart me instead — it works on most
of our agents.
